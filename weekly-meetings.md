# Weekly Meetings and Updates

Clear, consistent communication is one of the most important skills you will develop as a graduate researcher. This guide explains how 1-on-1 meetings with Rebecca work, what she needs from you each week, and how to make those meetings as useful as possible.

---

## The Weekly Update

Before your 1-on-1 each week, submit a written update using the template below. Send it to Rebecca at least a few hours before your meeting so she has time to read it.

The update is not busywork. It does three things:
1. Forces you to articulate what you actually did and why it matters
2. Gives Rebecca the context she needs to give you useful feedback
3. Creates a running record of your progress that you will thank yourself for later

**The most important question in the update is the last one: why does this connect to the bigger goal?** If you cannot answer it, that is a signal — either the work is not connected to the goal, or you do not have enough clarity on what the goal is. Both are worth surfacing in your meeting.

## How to Make Your 1-on-1 Useful

Come with an agenda. The update gives Rebecca context — the meeting is for decisions, feedback, and thinking together. Do not use meeting time to report things that could go in the written update.

**Good uses of 1-on-1 time:**
- Working through a decision you are stuck on
- Getting feedback on a specific piece of writing or analysis
- Discussing whether your current direction still makes sense
- Flagging a blocker that is slowing you down

**Things to do before the meeting:**
- Read your own update — know what you wrote
- Have your Overleaf, figures, or code open and ready to share if relevant
- Write down the 1-2 things you most need from Rebecca that week

## Time Tracking with Toggl

The lab uses Toggl to track how time is spent across research, coursework, and teaching responsibilities. This is not about surveillance — it is about helping you and Rebecca see whether your time allocation matches your priorities.

**How to set it up:**
1. Create a free account at toggl.com
2. Set up three main projects: Research, Courses, Teaching
3. Run the timer when you start a work block and stop it when you finish
4. At the end of each week, take a screenshot of your weekly summary and paste it into your update

You do not need to track every minute. Track your main work blocks and be consistent. A rough picture is more useful than nothing.

---

## Starting Something New

Before you begin any task that will take more than 2-3 days, write a short plan and check with Rebecca first. This is not about getting approval — it is about making sure you are solving the right problem before you invest a week in it. A 3-sentence message is enough:

1. What I am about to do
2. Why I am doing it
3. How it connects to the bigger goal

This habit will save you from the most common and painful graduate school mistake: working hard in the wrong direction for weeks before anyone notices.
