## Individual Development Plan (IDP)

An IDP is a structured planning conversation between you and Rebecca about where you are, where you want to go, and what you need to get there. The lab completes one every year.

An IDP is not a performance review. It is a planning tool — a way to make sure the work you are doing this year is actually moving you toward your goals.

Fill out the template below before each annual meeting, then share it with Rebecca so she can prepare.

### The Template

**Part I: Self-Assessment**

1. Which of your past accomplishments will contribute to your doctoral training? What skills are you bringing?
2. What areas of learning and growth would you like to develop in the next year? Which could be addressed in 3 months? In 6 or 9 months?

**Part II: Goals**

1. Long-term goals:
   - *Research goals:* What specific areas do you want to explore? What methods do you want to master?
   - *Professional development goals:* Public speaking, grant writing, manuscript preparation, teaching, mentoring?
   - *Career goals:* What position do you hope to hold 10 years from now? List first and second choice.
2. What significant factors are driving these goals — personal interests, geographic priorities, family commitments, financial objectives? Any concerns about paths that are otherwise of interest?

**Part III: Activities Towards Goals**

1. Research objectives (1–3): Courses, conference attendance, fellowship applications, anticipated publications with tentative titles.
2. Professional development objectives (1–3): Teaching, service, workshops.

**Part IV: Well-being (optional)**

Reflect on your research environment, work-life balance, and any other stressors or concerns.

**Part V: Anything Else?**

Anything not covered above that you want to discuss.

**Part VI: Agreed Action Plan for Next Year**

- Activities you and Rebecca agree you should pursue
- Funding status and backup plans
- Specific actions Rebecca can take to support your success
- Goals to revisit before your next meeting

### The Annual Meeting

Come with the template filled out. The meeting is a conversation, not a presentation — expect Rebecca to push on your goals and help you think through tradeoffs. Bring specific questions. Update the document after the meeting with anything you agreed on.

## What the PhD Arc Looks Like

Starting a PhD is disorienting even when it is going well. A PhD is a marathon, not a sprint. The arc typically looks like:

- **Year 1:** Coursework, reading, getting oriented. You are learning what the field is and where the gaps are.
- **Years 1–2:** Finding your research direction. You will read more than you build. This is normal.
- **Year 2–3:** Proposal and comprehensive exam. The formal transition from student to researcher.
- **Years 3–4:** Executing your dissertation research. Writing and publishing journal papers.
- **Year 4–5:** Finishing, writing the dissertation, and defending.

These timelines vary. What matters is continuous progress, not adherence to any particular schedule.

### Reflection Questions Worth Revisiting

Return to these at onboarding, before your IDP each year, and before your defense. Write actual answers, not mental notes.

**Your "Why"**
- What was the primary driving force behind pursuing a PhD?
- What experiences and skills are you bringing from your masters or professional career?
- What specific questions or problems are you most passionate about?
- When you successfully defend, how will that feel — and what does it represent?

**Your Goals**
- What does success look like in your first year? By the end of the program?
- Beyond the dissertation: publications, conferences, teaching, mentoring, grants?
- What specific skills do you want to develop, both research-specific and professional?
- What do you hope to be doing 1, 5, and 10 years after graduation?

**Your Path**
- What are your greatest strengths that will help you navigate a PhD?
- What are you most concerned or anxious about?
- What kind of support do you need from Rebecca? From peers and family?
- How will you maintain work-life balance?

There are no right answers. Be honest with yourself.
