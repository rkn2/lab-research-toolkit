# Using AI for Research: Worked Examples

This folder contains worked examples of how to use Claude for common research and advising tasks. Each example shows an actual prompt, explains why it was structured that way, and gives notes on how to adapt it for similar situations.

These are not templates to copy verbatim. The goal is to help you understand the reasoning behind each prompt — so that when you face a task not covered here, you can construct your own rather than guess.

## How to use these examples

1. **Read the research context.** If your situation is similar, read the whole example.
2. **Read the prompt.** Notice what it says explicitly and what it does not say.
3. **Read the "why" section before copying the prompt.** The reasoning matters more than the exact wording. A prompt that works for one task may fail for a slightly different one — knowing why it is written the way it is lets you adjust.
4. **Read "how to adapt"** and decide whether you need to modify anything for your case.

---

## A note on Claude's defaults

Claude is designed to be helpful and agreeable. For most research tasks, that default creates problems:

- It will soften criticism unless you tell it not to.
- It will fill in assumptions rather than flag them as gaps.
- It will produce output when it would be more useful to ask you questions first.

The prompts in these examples are structured to work around these defaults. Once you understand why each prompt is written the way it is, writing your own prompts becomes much more straightforward.
